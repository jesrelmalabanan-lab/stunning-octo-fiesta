A little about this collection

Every part tells a story.

This website gathers my reflections about the different dimensions of the self. Each one looks at a different part of my identity: the body I grew into, how I understand attraction and relationships, the faith that guides me, my relationship with possessions and resources, the person I am online, and my place in society.

These are not stories about having everything figured out. They are reflections on experiences, lessons, mistakes, values, and goals that continue to shape me. I am still learning about myself, and I know that some of my perspectives will continue to change as I grow.

My name is Jesrel Reiven Malabanan. I am from Socorro, Oriental Mindoro, and I am a BSABE student. This is my attempt to understand the person behind the different roles I have in life.

01
The body I grew into

My Physical Self

Where My Story Began

My story began in a way my family would remember. I was born feet-first at our family store, and a local manghihilot helped during the delivery. My mother was 38 years old at the time. I did not cry immediately after being born. For about five minutes, I remained quiet and started turning purplish, which frightened my mother. Eventually, I cried, and she wept with relief. It is a story I did not experience consciously, but it became part of how my family remembers the beginning of my life.

Growing Up in My Own Little Way

I began walking when I was around one year old and was potty-trained about six months later. By the age of two, I could already speak fluently. I eventually attended our barangay daycare. As a child, I was a very picky eater. Because our family had limited finances, taking vitamins regularly was not always possible. I also experienced a high fever when I was around two and was hospitalized. I still remember saying, “Ang lamig, tama na,” while a nurse wiped me with a cold towel.

I was short and thin as a child, and I even became part of a feeding program during elementary school. Despite that, I was physically active and enjoyed playing sports, especially basketball. I liked being outdoors and joining games with other children.

The Active Child I Used to Be

One incident during Grade 5 changed the way I looked at my physical appearance. While playing tag, I collided with another student. The accident left my face bruised and caused a deep cut on my upper lip. The injury nearly split my lip and eventually resulted in some facial asymmetry. It became one of the physical experiences I remember most from childhood.

Before Grade 6, I was also circumcised. Like many changes during childhood, it was another experience that became part of growing up.

The Unexpected Changes

When the pandemic began, my daily routine changed. I spent more time indoors, and the physical activities I used to enjoy gradually became less frequent. I lost some of the enthusiasm and discipline I once had for sports. Over time, my strength and stamina declined.

Around the age of 13, I started noticing that my eyesight was becoming blurry. At 15, I began wearing eyeglasses. Although they helped me see more clearly, I became hesitant to play contact sports and basketball because I worried about damaging or breaking them.

Discovering My Appearance During Adolescence

Like everyone going through puberty, I experienced changes in my body. I developed pimples, my voice changed, my posture and body began to mature, and facial hair started to appear. At first, I was afraid to trim my facial hair because I believed that shaving would make it grow thicker. Eventually, I learned that this was a misconception and became more comfortable grooming myself.

As I grew older, I became more conscious of my appearance. There were times when I focused too much on the things I thought were imperfections. I gradually realized that growing up also meant learning how to accept the features that make me who I am.

Learning to Accept the Person in the Mirror

My physical journey has not been perfect. My body has changed through childhood, accidents, illness, inactivity, and adolescence. There are things I appreciate and things I still want to improve. However, I have learned that my body deserves care rather than constant criticism.

I want to become healthier, regain my strength, and develop better habits. More importantly, I want to appreciate what my body has allowed me to experience. It carried me through every stage of my life, even during moments when I did not appreciate it enough.

My body is not something I should spend my life criticizing. It is something I should understand, care for, and appreciate.
02
More than just attraction

My Sexual Self

Knowing Who I Am

Understanding my sexual self has been a process of learning about my identity, feelings, attraction, relationships, and the way I treat other people. I identify as a straight man, and I am comfortable with that part of myself. However, I have learned that understanding sexuality involves more than simply knowing who I am attracted to. It also means understanding respect, boundaries, consent, and responsibility.

When I was younger, I had a very simple and innocent understanding of relationships. I once believed that babies were made when a husband and wife kissed. Whenever kissing scenes appeared on television, I would close my eyes because I felt shy watching them. Looking back, those childhood beliefs remind me how much my understanding has changed as I grew older.

Learning to Understand Others

As a child, I sometimes had judgmental views toward people who were different from what I was familiar with. I did not fully understand homosexuality and other expressions of sexuality. As I grew older, I learned to become more understanding and empathetic. I realized that people deserve respect regardless of their sexual orientation or identity.

I may not have the same experiences as everyone else, but I can still listen, learn, and treat people with dignity. Understanding others does not require me to have all the same beliefs or experiences. It requires me to recognize their humanity.

My Experiences With Love and Relationships

My first crush was when I was around seven years old. During elementary school, I experienced what I would now call puppy love. I had a classmate with whom I shared innocent moments, such as hugs and leaning on each other's shoulders. Even those simple gestures were enough to give me butterflies.

I also had a close female best friend. Our parents would sometimes tease us about getting married someday. There was even a moment when she leaned close enough that it seemed like we might share our first kiss, but I avoided it because of how I was raised and the beliefs I had at the time.

During the pandemic, I became close to a classmate online who liked me. We talked almost every day, but the connection did not develop into a relationship. I think we were both still immature and learning how to understand our feelings.

In high school, I pursued someone for around nine months. I became close to her family and even spent Christmas with them. I also developed a good relationship with her mother. Eventually, she asked me to stop pursuing her. When I later expressed my feelings, her response was that she was already comfortable with us being friends and questioned why I needed to say anything more. It was painful, but it taught me that feelings cannot be forced.

My first official relationship began after I met someone at an event in Batangas. She was from Cebu. We had known each other for about a month before entering a long-distance relationship that lasted nine months. Eventually, she asked to end the relationship. She told me that I cared too much and that she sometimes felt guilty because I was always understanding. Seeing her later share a photo booth picture with another guy was difficult, but it became another lesson about accepting situations I cannot control.

The Girl Who Made My Heart Skip a Beat

Later, I met a girl through church youth gatherings and a camp. We had actually seen each other before we properly knew each other's names. She was short, around two years older than me, and often wore dresses. We became friends, and I eventually confessed feelings that I had kept for a long time. She did not feel the same way.

She is now serving a full-time mission for the Church, and she has been away for about a year. I still have feelings for her, and part of me hopes that I will see her again and have the chance to express what I feel. At the same time, I understand that she has her own choices and priorities. I cannot expect her to feel the same way, and I know that I must respect whatever decision she makes.

Understanding Boundaries and Responsible Choices

These experiences taught me that relationships require more than attraction. They need honesty, communication, mutual effort, and respect. I have learned that consent matters, that no one owes another person affection, and that rejection should never be treated as permission to pressure someone.

I also learned that caring for someone does not mean losing myself in the process. I should be able to express my feelings while still respecting another person's boundaries. I want future relationships to be built on mutual understanding rather than pressure, assumptions, or one-sided effort.

I can choose to love someone, but I must also learn to respect their choice not to love me back.
03
Faith and finding my purpose

My Spiritual Self

Where My Faith Began

I was baptized into the Roman Catholic Church when I was one year old. As a child, however, I did not fully understand religion or why attending church was important. There were times when I disliked going to church and wondered why adults continued attending when it seemed like a routine. I also had questions about life that I could not answer. Why was I born? What was my purpose? What was I supposed to do with my life?

The Beginning of Something Different

When I was around eight years old, I met missionaries from The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Through them, I was introduced to teachings about Heavenly Father, Jesus Christ, the Plan of Salvation, the Atonement, the Book of Mormon, prayer, and scripture study.

At first, these ideas were simply new things I was learning. As time passed, they became more meaningful to me. My faith did not develop all at once. It grew through learning, asking questions, attending church, praying, and trying to understand how the teachings of Jesus Christ could apply to my life.

Discovering What I Believe In

My faith has helped me understand that life has a purpose beyond simply existing. I believe that I am a child of God and that Jesus Christ's teachings can guide the way I live. This belief gives me direction, especially when I face uncertainty or situations I do not fully understand.

I am still learning, and there are times when I have questions or struggle to understand why certain things happen. But I have learned that faith does not mean having every answer. Sometimes it means continuing to trust God while doing my best with what I know.

The Values That Shape Me

Some of the values I want to develop are humility, obedience, honesty, patience, charity, and service. I want these values to influence how I treat my family, friends, classmates, and even people I do not know personally.

One scripture that reminds me of the importance of service is Matthew 25:40, which teaches that when we serve others, we are also serving the Lord. It reminds me that kindness does not always need to be something grand. Sometimes it is shown through listening, helping, giving time, or simply being present for someone.

Faith, Fellowship, and Finding My Place

Church has also given me opportunities to build friendships and become part of a community. One meaningful experience was attending FSY 2025, which was my last FSY as a youth. Its theme, “Sight of Soul,” encouraged me to look beyond what is immediately visible and consider the person I am becoming.

Through youth activities and fellowship, I have experienced moments that strengthened my faith and reminded me that I do not have to walk through life alone. I have also learned that belonging to a community means contributing, supporting others, and growing together.

My Purpose and the Mission I Hope to Serve

One of my goals is to finish my degree in Agricultural and Biosystems Engineering. I want to become an engineer, support my mother, and eventually build a home for my family. These goals are important to me because I want to give back to the people who have supported me, especially my mother.

I also hope to serve a two-year full-time mission for the Church, ideally somewhere overseas. For me, serving a mission is not simply about wearing a name tag or traveling to another place. It is an opportunity to serve people, share my faith, grow spiritually, and learn to become more selfless.

Finding Meaning in the Things I Cannot Control

The passing of my father has been one of the experiences that shaped how I understand faith and family. He was one of my greatest supporters. He was not always expressive about affection, but his support was something I felt. Losing him made me realize how much I value the people who remain in my life.

There are prayers that do not receive the answers I expect, and there are situations that I wish could have happened differently. I am learning that faith does not remove sadness or uncertainty. Instead, it can help me find strength and meaning while going through them.

I once spent my childhood asking why I was here. Now, I want to spend my life discovering how I can make that life meaningful through faith, love, and service.

Still Walking With Him

The youth theme “Walk With Me,” connected to Moses 6:34, reminds me that spiritual growth is a journey. I do not need to become perfect overnight. I need to keep learning, making better choices, and trying to follow Jesus Christ each day.

My spiritual self is still developing. I know there will be moments when I feel strong in my faith and moments when I struggle. What matters is that I continue seeking, learning, and moving forward with the desire to become a better person.

04
Beyond what I own

My Material Self

Growing Up With What We Had

I did not grow up in a wealthy family. We had enough for our basic needs, but our resources were limited. There were times when we could not afford everything we wanted, and there were opportunities that I knew some other people could access more easily than we could.

Our situation was not always a matter of struggling every single day to survive, but I understood that money had limits. Growing up in that environment taught me to appreciate what we had and to understand that not everyone starts with the same opportunities.

Understanding Needs and Wants

As I grew older, I began to understand the difference between needs and wants. Needs are the things necessary for living and fulfilling responsibilities, while wants are things that may make life more comfortable or enjoyable but are not always necessary.

I do not think there is anything wrong with wanting nice things. However, I believe it is important to know when something is worth spending money on and when it is better to save. I want to become someone who can make practical decisions instead of spending simply because something looks appealing or because other people have it.

Material Possessions and Their Real Value

I have never viewed material possessions as the main measure of a person's worth. Clothes, gadgets, money, and other belongings can be useful, but they do not automatically make someone more important than others.

Some possessions are valuable because of the memories connected to them, while others are valuable because they help us accomplish something. I believe that the purpose of material things should be to support our lives, not to define our identity or determine how we treat people.

The Meaning of Financial Responsibility

Financial responsibility is something I want to improve as I become more independent. I want to learn how to budget, save, prioritize expenses, and use resources wisely. Even small amounts can matter when they are managed properly.

I also want to avoid unnecessary spending and become more prepared for unexpected expenses. I know that financial stability does not happen immediately. It requires discipline, patience, and decisions that consider both present needs and future goals.

What I Want to Achieve Beyond Material Wealth

One of my biggest goals is to finish my studies and become an Agricultural and Biosystems Engineer. I want to have a stable career that will allow me to support my mother and help provide for my family. I also hope to build a house someday, a place where my family can feel safe and comfortable.

These goals are not about becoming rich just for the sake of having money. I want financial stability because I know what it feels like to have limited resources. I want to have the ability to help the people I love, prepare for the future, and share what I can with others who may need assistance.

Living a Life Based on Values

My family and faith have taught me that a meaningful life cannot be measured through possessions alone. I want to remain grateful, generous, and content even as I work toward a better future. I do not want the desire for material things to make me forget the people and values that matter most.

I want to have enough to live comfortably, enough to provide for the people I love, and enough to share with those who need help.

More Than What I Own

My material self is connected to the way I understand money, possessions, opportunities, and responsibility. I know that I still have a lot to learn about managing resources, but I want to approach my future with discipline and gratitude.

For me, success is not simply owning expensive things. It is being able to live responsibly, support my family, fulfill my goals, and help others without losing sight of what truly matters.

My online identity and habits

My Digital Self

Getting to Know My Digital Self

Growing up, I witnessed how technology gradually became a bigger part of everyday life. What used to be something we accessed occasionally has become something we can carry almost everywhere. Today, I use digital devices for studying, communicating, entertainment, and discovering things that interest me.

My digital self is the version of me that people get to see through the internet. It includes my social media profiles, the things I share, my comments, the content I interact with, and even the way I communicate with others online. I know that my online identity is not the complete version of who I am. There are parts of my life I am comfortable sharing and others I prefer to keep private.

My Digital Identity

I would describe myself as someone who does not necessarily need to share everything happening in my life. There are times when I simply browse through social media without posting anything. I enjoy seeing what other people are doing, discovering interesting content, and keeping myself updated.

I do not believe I need to create a perfect image of myself just to gain approval. I would rather present myself naturally than constantly worry about how others perceive me. At the same time, I understand that I should be careful about what I share because not everything needs to be public.

My Digital Habits

As a college student taking up Agricultural and Biosystems Engineering, I find technology especially useful in my studies. There are lessons that require additional explanations, concepts I need to understand better, and problems that require me to look for different approaches. The internet gives me access to learning materials beyond what is discussed in class.

I have also used artificial intelligence tools such as ChatGPT and other AI platforms to assist me with academic tasks. I find them useful when I need explanations, examples, ideas, or help organizing information. However, I have learned that I should not automatically accept everything they provide. I still need to understand the topic, check the accuracy of the information, and make sure the work reflects my own understanding.

Outside academics, I spend time browsing social media, watching videos, and exploring content related to my interests. I enjoy reading stories, especially fiction and fantasy, and I also have an interest in games and other forms of online entertainment.

Of course, using my phone can sometimes become a distraction. There are moments when I intend to spend only a few minutes online but end up giving it more time than I planned. I am still working on managing these habits. I do not need to completely remove technology from my life. I want to become more disciplined and learn when to use it and when to take a break.

The Influence of Social Media on Me

When browsing through different platforms, I often see people sharing their achievements, travels, relationships, new purchases, and memorable experiences. These posts can be entertaining and inspiring, but they can also create an unrealistic picture of what life should look like.

It is easy to compare my own experiences with what other people choose to share. I have to remind myself that social media only shows selected moments. People usually share the things they want others to see, not necessarily everything they experience.

I also understand how likes, comments, and followers can affect confidence. Receiving positive reactions can make someone feel appreciated, while receiving little attention or negative comments can make someone question their worth. I believe these numbers should never become the basis of how I value myself.

Practicing Responsible Digital Citizenship

Being part of the digital world comes with responsibilities. I believe there is a real person behind every account. Being behind a screen does not excuse disrespectful behavior. I should be mindful of the words I use when commenting, replying to messages, or participating in online discussions.

I also have a responsibility to be careful with the information I share. False information can spread quickly, especially when people repost something without checking whether it is true. This is particularly important when using the internet for academic purposes. I should verify information through reliable sources rather than depending entirely on whatever appears in search results or AI-generated responses.

Protecting my privacy is another responsibility. I should be careful about sharing personal information, clicking unfamiliar links, and interacting with people I do not know. Not everything online is safe, and information shared publicly may reach people I never intended to see it.

Building a Healthier Relationship With Technology

I want to become more intentional about how I use technology. I want to improve the way I manage my time, especially when balancing academics and entertainment. I also want to use artificial intelligence as a learning aid rather than a replacement for my own effort. I should still read, analyze, understand, and think independently.

Most importantly, I want to maintain a balance between my online and offline life. Spending time with family, strengthening relationships, developing my interests, fulfilling responsibilities, and making time for my faith are things that cannot be completely replaced by anything I see on a screen.

I want my digital presence to be a reflection of the person I am trying to become, not a replacement for who I truly am.

More Than What Appears on the Screen

My digital self is an important part of my identity, but my online presence does not define my entire worth. I do not need the most impressive profile, the most reactions, or constant achievements to prove that I am living a meaningful life. I want to use technology with purpose, discipline, and respect.

The internet may give me a place to express myself, but it should never become the only place where I find my identity. Beyond the screen, I am still responsible for the person I choose to become.

Rights, responsibilities, relationships, and civic life

My Political Self

Understanding My Political Self

When I hear the word political, I often think of government, elections, and public officials. But in Understanding the Self, I have come to see that my Political Self is also about how I understand my rights and responsibilities, how I participate in my community, and how I relate to the institutions and issues that affect everyday life. It is connected to the way I see myself as a member of my family, school, church, community, and country.

I am still learning what it means to be an active and responsible citizen. I do not consider myself an expert on political issues, and I do not always take part in public discussions. Still, I recognize that society affects my opportunities, choices, and future. Being aware of what happens around me is therefore part of understanding who I am.

The Importance of Family, Friends, and Church

My understanding of society begins with the people closest to me. My family has a strong influence on the way I understand respect, responsibility, and decision-making. Growing up as the youngest among five siblings taught me that living with other people requires patience, consideration, and cooperation. My family's experiences have also made me aware that people do not all have the same resources and opportunities. These experiences shape how I look at issues that affect ordinary families.

My friends also influence how I see the world. Through conversations, shared experiences, and working together, I encounter opinions and perspectives that may be different from mine. I have learned that I do not have to agree with everyone, but I should be willing to listen and think carefully before forming my own judgment. At the same time, I need to be mindful of peer influence and make decisions that remain consistent with my values.

My church is another important part of my life. Being a member of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints has taught me the importance of service, compassion, honesty, and respect for others. Church activities and responsibilities have also helped me understand how people can work together toward shared goals. My faith influences my personal values, including how I treat people and how I understand my responsibility to contribute to the community. I also recognize that people may have different beliefs, and they deserve the same respect.

Family, friends, and church are important because they are not simply groups I belong to. They are part of the environment where I learn values, develop opinions, receive guidance, and practice living with others. They influence my Political Self, while I remain responsible for thinking for myself.

My Rights and Responsibilities

As a Filipino, I have rights that allow me to live with dignity, express my views, receive an education, practice my beliefs, and take part in society. I also have responsibilities that come with being part of a community. These include respecting the rights of others, following laws, being considerate, staying informed, and contributing to the common good.

I understand that rights and responsibilities go together. I cannot expect others to respect my opinions if I refuse to respect theirs. I should be able to express disagreement without insulting people, and I should recognize that my freedom does not give me permission to harm or silence someone else. These are principles I can practice in ordinary situations, not only during elections or political discussions.

How I Relate to Institutions and Social Issues

Different institutions affect my life in different ways. My family provides my first lessons about responsibility and belonging. My school gives me opportunities to learn, work with others, and prepare for my future. My church provides spiritual guidance and a community where I can serve. Local government and public institutions also affect the services and opportunities available to people in my community.

My work immersion at the Municipal Environment and Natural Resources Office in Socorro gave me a closer look at how a local government office serves the community. Through activities involving waste segregation, waste weighing, and observing the sanitary landfill, I saw that public concerns such as waste management and environmental protection require both institutional action and public cooperation. These issues are connected to people's health, surroundings, and quality of life.

As a student of Agricultural and Biosystems Engineering, I am also becoming more aware of issues involving agriculture, food production, water resources, and environmental sustainability. These are not distant topics because they affect farmers, families, and communities. I hope that what I learn in college will help me understand these concerns better and eventually contribute to practical solutions.

Civic Participation in Everyday Life

For me, civic participation does not begin and end with voting. Voting is an important responsibility when I become eligible, but participation can also mean learning about community concerns, following reliable information, taking part in school or community activities, respecting public spaces, and cooperating with efforts that benefit others.

I want to become more careful about the information I receive, especially online. Before believing or sharing claims about public issues, I should check where the information came from and whether it is supported by evidence. I should also make an effort to understand different sides of an issue instead of allowing popularity, pressure from friends, or emotional reactions to decide what I believe.

I may not always have the time or confidence to participate in every activity, but I can start by being informed, responsible, and willing to help where I can. Small actions matter when they are done with consideration for other people and the community.

Forming My Own Views

The people and institutions around me influence my beliefs, but I do not want to accept every opinion simply because it comes from someone I respect or belong with. My family, friends, and church are important to me, yet I also need to reflect, ask questions, and examine information. I can value their guidance while developing my own understanding of social issues.

I also want to remember that people can disagree and still treat one another with dignity. A person's political opinion, financial situation, religion, or background should not be the only basis for judging their worth. Listening does not always mean agreeing. It means recognizing that other people have experiences that may differ from mine.

I want to understand my rights, fulfill my responsibilities, and take part in my community without losing respect for the people whose experiences and opinions are different from mine.

More Than My Place in Society

My Political Self is shaped by the relationships and institutions that matter in my life, as well as by my growing awareness of the society I belong to. My family, friends, and church help shape my values. My education and experiences introduce me to social concerns. My rights remind me that I have a voice, while my responsibilities remind me that my actions affect other people.

I am still learning how to participate more actively and how to form well-informed views. I do not need to pretend that I know everything. What matters is that I remain willing to learn, question information, respect others, and contribute in ways that I can.

As I continue my studies and prepare for my future profession, I hope to use what I learn in ways that are useful to the people around me. I want to be someone who understands that being part of society is not only about what I can receive from it, but also about what I can responsibly give back.
